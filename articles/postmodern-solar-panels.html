Postmodern Solar Panels

By Jordan Ellis · 12 March 2025

For decades, solar hardware apologised for itself. Panels were tucked on the least-photogenic roof plane, framed in utility black, and treated as a necessary scar. Postmodern solar flips that instinct: the array becomes ornament. Colour, rhythm, and iridescence join the façade conversation instead of hiding from it.

Once cells can shimmer like jewellery, composition matters as much as kilowatts. A cream plaster wall can carry a grid of panels the way a Memphis façade once carried laminate colour blocks. Soft afternoon light turns each module into a shifting tile—blue one moment, violet the next—without needing any brand mark to announce itself.

Courtyard installations push the idea further. Freestanding modules on slim pedestals read as sculpture first and infrastructure second. In our fictional catalogue, that line of thinking lands as the Vanity Solar Panel—a façade-mounted piece that treats generation as a happy side effect of looking deliberately beautiful.

Up close, the cell grid is abstract geometry. No readable markings, no dashboard of status icons—just soft specular colour. That restraint is part of the postmodern joke: technology that refuses to look technical.
